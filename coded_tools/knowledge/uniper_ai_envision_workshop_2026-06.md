## The day in one paragraph

Senior Uniper leaders spent one very active day in a room whose walls were filled with hand-drawn
pictures. They sharpened the focus question, built a shared understanding of AI (history, the AI
Fluency Mountain, Cynefin and the Knowledge Funnel, the "Tapestry" of five success areas), described
the strategic outcomes and the "dividends" they expect, took an honest stocktake of what is done,
in progress and missing, drew a shared vision two years out, worked in small groups on five
transformation deep dives, named the critical success factors still missing, and closed with an
After Action Review. Fabienne's closing line: "This is a journey for all of us."

Flow of the day (agenda order):
1. Focus Question
2. AI Foundation: History of AI, AI Fluency Mountain, Strategic Framework (Cynefin + Knowledge Funnel), the Tapestry
3. Strategic Outcomes
4. Value Dividends
5. Current Stocktake (done / doing / to be done, plus technical debt)
6. Shared Vision (stakeholder voices, employee experience, core values, capability stack)
7. Transformation Deep Dives (five groups)
8. Critical Success Factors (overarching)
9. Shared Messages
10. After Action Review and Reflection

## 1. Focus Question

Draft: "How does Uniper become a digitally enabled agentic enterprise capable of thriving in the
complexity of the energy market, accelerating its transition to a high-performing company?"

Participants tested the wording with questions: Is digitally enabled just AI? Does this fit together?
Define high-performing. AI and energy? What's the stack? These were not complaints — the group was
checking whether the language was load-bearing before building on it. (Margin notes on the drawing:
"leveraging AI agents 24/7", "high level of automation".)

Two changes: "digitally enabled" is more of a support layer, so "agentic enterprise" should lead; and
the move away from fossil-fuel dependency had to be visible, so "decarbonized" was added.

Final focus question: "How does Uniper become an agentic enterprise capable of thriving in the
complexity of the energy market, accelerating its transition to a decarbonized, digitally enabled,
and high-performing company?"

## 2. AI Foundation

### History of AI — the past decade

A drawn timeline of milestones:
- March 2016: AlphaGo's "Move 37" — the spark of machine intuition (a move no human would choose, and right).
- June 2017: "Attention is all you need" — birth of the Transformer.
- December 2019: DeepMind's MuZero — the first latent world model.
- June 2020: OpenAI releases GPT-3 — the scaling hypothesis.
- December 2020: DeepMind AlphaFold 2 — structural biology's holy grail.
- November 2022: OpenAI launches ChatGPT — the alignment breakthrough.
- March 2023: GPT-4 — the multimodal frontier.
- 2024: Google DeepMind world model (Genie, generative interactive) and OpenAI o1 (System 2 thinking).
- Early 2026: world models (e.g. Waymo) and Anthropic's Mythos Preview — autonomous agentic defense;
  it changed how the world thinks about patching systems.

James, who tracks benchmarks with his commercial engineering team, said the autonomous reasoning
hours score for Mythos-level frontier models has reached 40 — so steep the chart barely fits.
Implication: a teenager anywhere now has penetration capability that six months ago required a
government-trained crack team. Pedro confirmed a cybersecurity working group is already active.
Urgency: match the pace of the threat with new risk-based responses.

Takeaway: the landscape changes faster than any multi-year roadmap. Plan in six-month sprints and
assume the technology is significantly more capable before each plan is finished.

### The AI Fluency Mountain

A drawn mountain with seven levels (capability maturity rises as you climb):
1. Prompt — AI answers from pre-trained knowledge and prompt history.
2. RAG — AI drafts a response using external documents provided to it.
3. Skill — consistent, predictable responses by following precise directions, expert rules and tools.
4. Agent — reasoning loops and approved sources to pursue open-ended goals within guardrails.
5. System — high autonomy across the enterprise, orchestrating many agents and systems for long-running workflows (examples drawn: Waymo, agentic SOC, self-driving networks).
6. Discovery — interacts with the real world to generate original insights beyond human limits.
7. Learning — continuously evolves by analysing and re-engineering its own architecture.

Participants wrote their name and what they had built on coloured flags and placed them on the
mountain. The surprise: flags clustered much higher than expected. The organisation assumed AI
experimentation was sparse and early-stage; in fact people across the business had been quietly
building — sometimes in their own time, on weekends, because no resources were ring-fenced.
Quote: "I saw that on YouTube and built my own version. The sharing of this stuff has never been
easier. It's almost trivial to say, here's my thing."

Examples visible on the flags: a financial-planner skill, RAG summaries, a skill to optimise
consistency checks, published skills and statements of work, a "grill me" skill, an early-warning
project risk app, an agent extracting lessons learned, the ALEX AI learning coach, gamified learning
with AI, an autonomous forecasting and resourcing agent, a multi-agent framework for trading,
published multi-agent apps, a self-built personal vault.

### Strategic framework: Cynefin + Knowledge Funnel

- Cynefin (Dave Snowden) sorts situations into: Clear (tightly constrained, sense–categorise–respond,
  best practice), Complicated (governing constraints, sense–analyse–respond, good practice),
  Complex (enabling constraints, probe–sense–respond, emergent practice), Chaotic (no constraints,
  act–sense–respond, novel practice).
- Knowledge Funnel (Roger Martin): knowledge flows from mystery → heuristic → algorithm/code.

Together: in the complex domain AI helps explore mysteries; in the complicated domain AI assists
experts, who share their heuristics as skills; regulation and policy can also become skills, so
skills combine rules and judgement.

- Digitize and automate the "Clear" system — turn policy into constraints. Convert policy Word
  documents to Markdown under version control (e.g. GitHub) so agents can ingest them as operational
  skills. The more precise the skills, the less likely the workflow tips into chaos.
- Codify the "Complicated" context — reward experts for publishing heuristics as AI skills. Example:
  an invoice skill saying "when you get an invoice from this person, ask these questions, do not pay
  them, talk to Christian." Experts should turn their expertise into a product, not a service. The
  most successful professionals will be those who containerise their wisdom so an agent can do the
  heavy lifting.

### The Tapestry — five integrated components for agentic enterprise success

A drawn grid with Culture in the centre and Data, ROI, Risk, People around it. A checklist for Uniper.

- Culture — foster a generative environment. Ron Westrum's model: pathological cultures shoot the
  messenger, bureaucratic ones send the message to "proper channels", generative ones train the
  messenger. Key question drawn: "How do you deliver bad news in your organisation?" Surface
  low-level concerns; failure leads to blameless inquiry. Emerging evidence: high-speed AI work can
  isolate people and cause burnout, so some organisations re-establish face-to-face work for
  co-creation (e.g. programming) to keep cohesion, psychological safety and trust.
- Data — data as a product. Agents are only as good as their data. Democratise data beyond silos,
  build an active data foundation and a "semantic fabric", make data quality the producer's
  responsibility. Non-negotiable prerequisite for real-time intelligence.
- Risk — the layered "harness". Frontier LLMs are non-deterministic "creatures" needing deterministic
  human-built constraints. Drawn as concentric rings: LLM at the core; runtime (orchestration loop,
  output parsing, error handling, prompt construction, state and context management); capabilities
  (tools, memory); outer ring safety & scale (guardrails, verification loops, observability, audit).
  Human-in-the-loop (HITL) for some decisions, human-over-the-loop (HOTL) with exception monitoring
  for others; scope containment, least-privilege access, audit trails.
- People — upskilling and role fluidity. Silos and job descriptions collapse (e.g. product managers
  and developers merge). Use the ALEX AI learning coach; redefine jobs around capabilities; formally
  recognise "skills authorship" when someone turns a rule of thumb into an AI skill.
- ROI — modernise value tracking. Project accounting misses the compounding value of the AI
  "flywheel". Move to "Value Loop Accounting", a value taxonomy separating efficiency, effectiveness
  and transformation, and tiered investment governance matched to value horizons.

## 3. Strategic Outcomes

Drawn as a list next to a big blue exclamation mark: robust portfolio for differentiation; highly
generative (learning) culture; engagement & management score 10/10; CFO signs off risk report;
quick at capturing new market opportunities; elimination of unplanned outages; rich pipeline;
AI-supported decision making; real-time systems and data processing; market price capturing (data
every second); traders in the loop; monetising our assets; product owners = data owners; zero work
around; supporting cross-functional collaboration; autonomous plants respond to market signals.

Narrative: operationally, no unplanned outages thanks to intelligent forecasting and assets that
predict their own maintenance; autonomous plants respond automatically to market signals; physical
AI may eventually include robots and sensors handling manual operations and "turns" across the asset
base. Administratively, full automation of key workflows; from a periodic Medium Term Plan (MTP) to a
continuous real-time financial timeline; financial and product-variation approvals collapse from
weeks to one day; touchless risk reports signed off by the CFO under strict human-over-the-loop.
Culturally, cross-functional collaboration, outcome focus over rigid process, employees moving from
non-value-add tasks to creative problem solving with agents, capability-driven career paths.

## 4. Value Dividends

Four dividend streams forming a flywheel (drawn around a central picture of gears and robots):
- Development Velocity ("Imagine how!"): constraints become enablers, more effectiveness,
  productivity, assets built, skills built, core skill coverage, "bespoke at speed", built by us.
- Assets / Operational: build assets with speed and quality, strategic planning, intelligent
  forecasting, fewer unplanned outages, respond to real-time signals; market prices every second for
  real-time P&L; physical AI in the asset base.
- Administrative / Financial: full automation, continuous MTP timeline, standard documents and
  product-variation approvals in one day, touchless CFO risk reports.
- Culture / People: 10/10 engagement and management scores, information flow, low-level concerns
  surfaced, stakeholder/customer satisfaction, joint experiments, employee attractiveness,
  learning-driven effectiveness, focus on outcome rather than process.

Mike asked the framing question: at what point does the flywheel start? Answer from the room: "We're
not at the point yet where we can really think about the flywheel. But we need to set the basis —
and the eye is looking up there." Not a failure: an accurate reading that investment sits close to
the tipping point, but not yet past it.

## 5. Current Stocktake

The most candid part of the day. The wall was split into three zones (Done / Doing / To be done),
filled without a filter. In the middle of the drawing: a bridge between COO and CCO data, and an
"ALEX — next week" bubble.

### Done (richer than expected)
- Data platform for LNG and energy markets: collects and quality-checks fundamental market data,
  AI discovery via Copilot, forecast models for market prices; modular and scalable.
- COO data platform with live asset connectivity: real-time data from most European assets;
  condition monitoring in production since 2015–16. Started in 2016 with standardisation — getting
  20 power plants from 20 different companies onto one data architecture.
- Unstructured data integration: investigation reports, supplier bulletins, lessons learned,
  engineering catalogues searchable alongside structured data. "We are into everything that matters."
- Multi-agent framework on the COO platform: early production, end-to-end queries across weather,
  market price, SAP and engineering catalogue data to produce risk assessments; not yet open to the
  wider organisation.
- Angel: commercial multi-agent system — specialised agents debate market positions, moderated by a
  deep-reasoning "portfolio manager" model; shown to the board in October 2024; not yet back-tested.
- Gamified AI learning: AI-built games teaching gas and power trading, Uniper strategy and commercial
  fundamentals, built by James Brady's team in hours; used by Sarah to onboard junior traders and by IT.
- ALEX: AI Learning Executive coach by Jenny Wichmann's HR team, ready for the LT200 the following
  week; gender neutral, any language.
- Cybersecurity operations centre across OT and IT — rare in the industry, hard won.

### Doing — needs acceleration
- Data democratisation: data exists but is not safely accessible; ownership ambiguity makes teams
  build their own copies.
- Embedding AI in data platforms: quality assurance, voice control, automated model selection — no
  flywheel momentum yet.
- Data fabric and comprehensive catalogue: Snowflake instances and catalogues exist, but no unified
  semantic layer.
- Standardising the agentic framework: the COO framework is powerful but inaccessible to others.
- Cyber threat monitoring with AI: a Glasswing working group is active; full OT/IT deployment is
  ahead and urgent.

### To be done — the honest gaps
1. Profitability at asset and customer level. Uniper does not know which individual assets or
   customers are profitable; hedging and commercial decisions are made at portfolio level by
   necessity. Pedro: "We do not have a clue what the revenues and profitability of the single asset
   are. The data exists across multiple systems. It has simply never been assembled this way."
2. Straight-through processing. Almost every end-to-end process has manual steps; Celonis could
   analyse performance, but manual interfaces block it.
3. Data fabric. Without a unified semantic model, agents cannot traverse the organisation. Naming
   (catalogue, fabric, intelligent layer) and architecture must be aligned before the next board talk.

The COO–CCO data bridge was the day's sharpest example of a structural problem disguised as a
technology problem: "We are struggling to get that data. We want to be able to sell in the market as
soon as the plant ramps down — but we rely on their data and we can't get to it quickly enough. We
need to build that bridge." Diagnosis: the technology exists; missing are clear ownership, service
levels and accountability for data quality. People resist APIs partly because they don't want to be
"put on the hook" with customers to look after. Prescription: force clarity — quality follows
accountability.

### The technical debt conversation
Mike: "Are we reducing technical debt quicker than we were two years ago?" Honest answer: yes, but
reactively, not systematically. AI can now analyse legacy code and write documentation for forgotten
systems. Uniper has 700+ applications, only about 200 essential; most of the rest are off-the-shelf
software customised beyond recognition. Path: conscious, AI-assisted retire/rebuild/replace
decisions. Missing: a ranked inventory of technical debt by business risk and flexibility value.

- Operational agility: "easy to work with", "they respond quickly."
- Deep trust: "I can trust these guys!", "Their products meet my needs!"

- Efficient & Authentic — speed through automation, never at the cost of human connection and integrity.

- Radical Change & Co-determination — bold change, executed with the workforce.
- Trustworthy & Risk-Taking — reliable systems make calculated risks safe.
- Simplify & Comply — compliance baked into agent guardrails, not delays for humans.
- Entrepreneurial & Focused — act like owners, stay focused on your contribution.

### The capability stack — what gets built (pyramid, top to bottom)
- Integrated process landscape: touchless, straight-through, no manual steps in critical processes.
- Democratic data products: owned, quality-assured, role-accessible, connected.
- Seamless approval and decision processes: agents and humans at market speed.
- All functions/businesses at Level 5 on the AI Mountain.
- Everyone is agent engaged.
- Base: leaders' change story and change management.

## 7. Transformation Deep Dives

Small groups took five areas and made "a plan for a plan": what is needed to start, what comes next,
and critical success factors (CSFs). Each group presented; the room said what they liked or loved,
what was unclear, next steps and who owns them after the room.

### 7.1 Technical Debt and Legacy Modernisation
Start with a discipline Uniper lacks: a comprehensive catalogue of all legacy systems, with cost and
value of eliminating each. Then use AI to analyse code, document, redesign processes and retire
applications step by step. Outcome: resilience, security, reliability, future agility.
Why it failed before: "We deviate quite often — something new comes in, we leave the existing work,
and then it becomes the never-ending book."
CSFs: common roadmap understanding between technical experts and business; consistent senior
leadership support; tolerance for failure; protected time and focus; AI-assisted analysis; conscious
build-vs-buy. The room loved that failure tolerance was named explicitly as a CSF. Added: communicate
it so the whole company understands, not only IT. Wall notes: "teenager cleaning room", "staying
lean, great vision", "ask for help", "plan for the plan", "it's on all of us to do."

### 7.2 Bridging — Connecting Data
Two clusters: customer profitability and asset profitability. Three steps: gather and allocate data;
use it for business insight; take action and make money. Bring the commercial platforms, asset
platforms and business controlling onto the same data. Decide build vs buy. Instead of building a new
database, ask an agent to allocate revenues and costs to an asset or customer — the data already
exists. Then democratise the data for hedging, commercial and technical decisions; integrate ongoing
contribution-margin and end-to-end accountability projects. Teams have strong business understanding
but lack technical depth. Past attempts at asset-level profitability failed by aiming for perfection:
80–90% of the value comes from simple stuff; focus on the top 20%.
Quote: "The companies that have worked this out are using it for more aggressive sales growth. They
know where they'll make margin ... Before, it was always an argument in a spreadsheet."
CSFs: overcome a complex data landscape; align with Orion P2 and use AI to go faster; inject
technical and analytical people; share information across systems via APIs everyone sticks to ("API
the Amazon way"), simple systems, documented process models.
Next step: Pedro accepted to convene a follow-up with commercial, controlling and IT. The room's
instruction: don't let it die in governance, don't let it stay a hobby project.

### 7.3 The AI Learning Journey — Beyond ALEX
ALEX is a beginning, not an endpoint. Next: evolve job descriptions, career paths, resource planning
and leadership behaviour for an AI-augmented operating model (wall: tone from the top, BoM → LT200
→ LT200 teams; understand demand, define resourcing, career plan).
Fabienne: "Moving Uniper from a task-based company to a capability-based company. Job descriptions
should not describe tasks. They should describe the capabilities a person brings — including which AI
agents they can deploy, what they have built, and how they contribute to the organisation's
collective agentic workforce."
CSFs: leaders model AI use publicly; access to data via connectors is non-negotiable (agents without
data lose trust); the programme must be pull, not push.

### 7.4 Integrated Process Landscape
Not another process programme but the foundation: without straight-through processing agents can't
run autonomously, data quality stays suspect, accountability stays invisible. Three blocks: a Level 1
process map identifying where manual steps hurt most; first improvements with genuinely engaged
process owners; in parallel, culture work on risk aversion. Celonis is used at maybe 20–30% of its
potential: AI recommends simplifications, Celonis measures before and after — continuous, not periodic.
CSFs: process-owner buy-in, not top-down; prioritise admin-heavy, high-frequency processes; reduce
risk aversion; align end-to-end process ownership with the new business lines; incentivise
simplification; data and AI first in every new investment.

### 7.5 Data Fabric — "One Uniper" Data Fabric
The most foundational. One logical Uniper data fabric regardless of where data lives — agents must
go where they need without hitting walls. Hans committed: Digital Technology acts as "Customer Zero"
and owns any data domain nobody else claims, because ownership forces quality. Data is owned by
Uniper, not functions. An embedded AI provisioning layer — a living, queryable semantic model, not a
static catalogue — plus an intelligent layer with skills management and an agent layer above. Every
new tech investment must contribute to the fabric: no more silos by design. James's nuance: not every
use case fits one platform; technical teams should align language and architecture and bring options
with pros and cons to the board. "There is no silver bullet. There are only conscious choices."
Wall: "Data democratisation — it's Uniper's data!", catalogue, easy to access and use (UX), quality
assurance with ownership assignment, embedded AI with standard tech stack and skills management.
The room loved: very clear; Customer Zero — lead by example.

## 8. Critical Success Factors (overarching, what was missing)
- ESG — environmental, social and governance considerations.
- Implementation follow-up — stay aligned, keep critical mass, track progress without external
  consultants ("do it yourself").
- Culture — plans were "very light on culture compared to our vision".
- Resource constraints — the organisation is under pressure with a lot of ongoing change.
- Workers' representatives — engage them for co-determination and alignment.

## 10. After Action Review and Reflection

What went well: incredible engagement of senior leaders; the AI Mountain as grounding; the moving,
active format kept everyone awake; fun, energy, laughter; people loved the hand-drawn visuals.
Even better if: more concrete, tangible actions and next steps; more focus and realism given how much
transformation is already happening.

Fabienne's reflection: "This was a great workshop. Thanks to all the participants for the great
discussion. This was a fantastic environment for brainstorming, to be concrete, and to get it going.
For some today was the starting point. I feel upskilled... a lot. There's a lot more work to do. But
what we've seen today is that this is a journey for all of us. This has to be done by everyone in this
company and that is actually a great thing to do!"
